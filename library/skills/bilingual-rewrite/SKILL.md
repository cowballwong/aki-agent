# Bilingual rewrite

## The thing that makes this different from translation

Hong Kong business writing has its own register. A literal translation reads
as though it came from somewhere else, and the reader notices immediately.

- Traditional characters, always.
- Hong Kong usage, not Mainland usage. 「電郵」not「郵件」,「軟件」not
  「軟體」, and local terms of art for professions — 「工程師」「則師」
  「則例」「屋宇署」as the person themselves would write them.
- Business Chinese for business documents; that is written Chinese, not
  Cantonese as spoken. Cantonese phrasing is right for a message to a friend
  or a site foreman, and wrong in a submission letter.
- Keep proper nouns, statutory names, form numbers and standards in the form
  they are officially used, usually English.

## Producing a bilingual document

When both languages appear in one document, keep them **side by side or in
matched blocks**, so a reader can check one against the other. Do not
interleave sentence by sentence.

## What to flag

If a term has no clean equivalent — a legal term, a defined contract term —
keep the original in brackets after your rendering rather than choosing a
translation that changes its meaning. Say that you did.
