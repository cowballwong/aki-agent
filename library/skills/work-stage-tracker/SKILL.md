# Work stage tracker

## What to keep

For each project: current stage, what closing it requires, what is
outstanding, and who owes it.

Use whichever stage vocabulary the user's practice uses — RIBA, the client's
own gateways, or a bespoke list. Ask once and then be consistent; a tracker
that renames stages halfway is worse than none.

## Moving a stage on

A stage closes when its deliverables exist and the client has said so, not
when the date passes. Show both: what is done, and whether sign-off was
actually received. "Assumed approved" is the state that produces disputes
later, so name it when it applies.

## What to surface

- what the next gate needs that does not yet exist
- anything being designed ahead of an approval it depends on — that is a
  commercial risk, and it is worth saying out loud
- fee stages that have been passed without being invoiced
