# Variation valuation

## Start with the instruction

Find it, quote it, and date it. A variation without a written instruction is
the first thing to raise — not the last, and not silently.

## Valuing

In the order the contract itself sets out, which is usually:

1. contract rates, where the work is the same character and conditions
2. contract rates adjusted, where character or conditions changed
3. fair rates, where neither applies
4. daywork, where nothing else can

Say which basis you used **for each item**, and why. A valuation that does
not say its basis cannot be checked, so it gets challenged in full.

## Include

Time as well as money where the instruction affects the programme, and say if
you do not have enough to assess it.

## Never

Do not net off an omission against an addition without showing both. Do not
apply a rate from another project. Do not present an estimate as a valuation
— label it.
