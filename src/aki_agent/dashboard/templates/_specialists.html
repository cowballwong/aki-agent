Narrow roles the assistant can hand a bounded piece of work to. Each one runs on its own, reads what it needs, and reports back — so deep work does not fill up the conversation you are having.

A specialist is read-only unless you say otherwise, never sends anything to anyone, and treats everything it reads as information rather than instructions. Those three rules are added to every brief automatically.

Neither was true. `sentinel.is_on()` is read in four places and every one of them only prints something; `review()` deliberately ignores it, which is right — somebody who asks for a check has asked for a check. It gates something as of 2026-08-23, and exactly one thing: a draft is checked before it is put in front of you. The maintainer asked for the switch to default on, having been told what it costs. Drafts only, and that is the whole design. Every check is a full model consultation, so putting one in front of every outgoing message would roughly double what a scheduled run costs — for questions and waiting items, which are the assistant *asking* rather than asserting, that buys nothing. A draft is the one thing here that goes out in your name. #} {% if built_in_on %}

Every draft is checked before you see it, and the verdict sits with the draft on Today so it is in front of you at the moment you decide. It reports; it never rewrites, and it never holds a draft back — a draft that could not be checked still reaches you, saying so.

You can also ask at any time: say “check this” in a session, or run the check command.

One model call per draft. Nothing else is checked automatically — not questions, not messages — because those are the assistant asking rather than asserting.
